# Evidence method

Keep source, assertion, observation and interpretation separate. A claim points to one or more immutable source IDs; a capability points to claims; a project or employment event points to both. Multiple rewritten resumes and mirrored repositories are not independent corroboration.

Evidence labels: observed platform fact; artifact-supported content; organization-corroborated context; documentary-supported copy; reported validation; first-party assertion; disputed assertion; not established. Documentary support does not imply issuer authentication. Activity labels independently distinguish historical completion, completed artifact, current as of review, in progress, proposal, future goal, unknown validity and unknown current status.

Repository evidence is pinned to recorded commits. A source file proves that code/artifact exists, not that it works, is original, is solely authored, is novel, or has the advertised physical effect. Reported test results remain reported unless rerun. No source code was executed for this initial capability analysis.

Keep dates exactly as stated, with source date and date precision. Never infer uninterrupted employment from overlapping summary spans. Never turn an undated Present into today's employment status. Never attribute a project-wide metric to a person.

Research stages are distinct: draft; prepared submission; submitted with receipt; revision requested; under review with dated correspondence; accepted with acceptance notice; published with publisher record; withdrawn/rejected with notice. A preprint and a manuscript ID are not acceptance or publication.

Raw originals, tax/identity documents, signatures, private journal correspondence and reference contacts stay in the local restricted intake. Public hub records carry source IDs and necessary bounded summaries. Existing public legacy materials have been preserved; preservation does not approve every old claim for future reuse.

Collection remains open. Review is staged: inventories and hashes; text extraction; relevant visual review; claim classification; corroboration; conflict resolution; bounded synthesis; final derivative documents. Media sampling and source extraction are not a claim of exhaustive audiovisual review. The archive must remain useful even when a claim cannot yet be resolved.
